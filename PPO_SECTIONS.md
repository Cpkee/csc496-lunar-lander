## 1. Implementation Details for PPO

The Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) algorithm was implemented following the framework established by Schulman et al. (2017), with a dual-network architecture consisting of a policy network (actor) and a value network (critic). The implementation features a specialized `PPOMemory` class that stores complete trajectories including states, actions, rewards, log probabilities, and value estimates, which enables the computation of Generalized Advantage Estimation (GAE) for improved advantage calculations. The core innovation of PPO lies in its clipped surrogate objective function, which constrains policy updates by limiting the probability ratio between old and new policies to the range [1-ε, 1+ε], where ε is the clipping parameter (default 0.1). During training, the agent collects trajectories of a specified horizon length (default 128 steps), computes GAE advantages with normalization for stability, and then performs multiple epochs (default 4) of minibatch stochastic gradient descent on the collected data. The complete loss function combines three components: the clipped policy objective, a value function loss (MSE between predicted and target returns), and an entropy bonus to encourage exploration. Both networks are optimized using the Adam optimizer with a learning rate of 2.5e-4, and gradients are clipped to a maximum norm of 0.5 to prevent exploding gradients. The implementation supports both stochastic action selection during training and deterministic selection during evaluation, with the policy network outputting action logits that are converted to probabilities via Softmax and sampled from a Categorical distribution.

## 2. Analysis Paragraph for PPO

The PPO implementation demonstrates several key characteristics that make it well-suited for the LunarLander-v2 environment. The algorithm's sample efficiency is enhanced through its multi-epoch training approach, which allows the agent to learn from the same trajectory data multiple times with different random minibatch shuffling, effectively extracting more information from each collected sample compared to single-pass methods. The clipped surrogate objective provides inherent stability by preventing large policy updates that could destabilize training, while the GAE mechanism reduces variance in advantage estimates compared to Monte Carlo methods while maintaining lower bias than simple TD(0) estimates. However, the on-policy nature of PPO means that collected trajectories cannot be reused after policy updates, requiring fresh data collection for each optimization cycle, which can be computationally expensive for long-horizon tasks. The hyperparameter sensitivity was evident during experimentation, where adjustments to the learning rate (reduced from 2.5e-4 to 1e-4), horizon length (increased from 128 to 256), and network architecture (expanded from [8,128,64] to [8,256,128]) showed measurable impacts on training stability and convergence speed. The algorithm's performance on LunarLander-v2 reveals a learning curve that typically shows rapid initial improvement followed by more gradual refinement, with the agent learning to balance thrust control, orientation, and landing precision. The entropy coefficient plays a crucial role in maintaining exploration throughout training, preventing premature convergence to suboptimal policies, while the value loss coefficient balances the relative importance of policy improvement versus value function accuracy.

## 3. Summary of Findings and Future Directions

The implementation and evaluation of PPO on the LunarLander-v2 environment revealed several important findings regarding algorithm performance, hyperparameter sensitivity, and practical considerations. The algorithm successfully learned to solve the environment, demonstrating the effectiveness of the clipped surrogate objective and GAE for stable policy gradient learning. Key observations include the importance of careful hyperparameter tuning, particularly for learning rates and network architectures, where modest adjustments led to improved training stability and sample efficiency. The multi-epoch training approach proved valuable for data efficiency, though the on-policy requirement limits scalability for very long training runs. The implementation also highlighted the trade-offs between exploration (entropy bonus) and exploitation (policy improvement), where maintaining sufficient exploration throughout training is critical for avoiding local optima. For future work, several promising directions emerge: implementing distributed PPO variants to parallelize trajectory collection and reduce wall-clock training time, exploring adaptive hyperparameter schemes that adjust learning rates and clipping parameters based on training progress, and investigating hybrid approaches that combine PPO's stability with off-policy methods for improved sample efficiency. Additionally, extending the implementation to support continuous action spaces through parameterized action distributions, implementing more sophisticated network architectures such as attention mechanisms for complex state representations, and developing automated hyperparameter optimization pipelines would further enhance the algorithm's applicability and performance. The diagnostic tools framework, while currently requiring fixes for compatibility, represents a valuable direction for understanding training dynamics and identifying optimization bottlenecks, which could inform more intelligent hyperparameter selection and algorithm improvements.
